# Behavioral & resume prep for system design interviews

System design interviews don't just test architecture — they test **leadership, ownership, and impact**, especially for senior and staff roles. This companion doc helps you prepare the non-technical half of the loop.

## What interviewers look for (beyond the diagram)

| Signal | What it means | How to show it |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Ownership | You drive outcomes, not just tasks | Talk about what *you* owned, decisions *you* made, and results *you* delivered |
| Technical leadership | You uplevel others and set direction | Describe tech specs you wrote, RFCs you drove, or standards you set |
| Conflict resolution | You disagree and commit productively | Share a time you disagreed with a peer/lead, how you resolved it, and the outcome |
| Ambiguity | You operate without a full spec | Describe a project with unclear requirements and how you narrowed scope |
| Impact | Your work moved a metric | Quantify: latency reduced, cost saved, incidents prevented, users served |

## Common behavioral questions (with answer structure)

Use **STAR** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) — keep it to ~2 minutes.

### 1. "Tell me about a time you had a technical disagreement."
- **Situation:** what you were building and who disagreed
- **Task:** the decision that needed to be made
- **Action:** how you presented data, listened, found common ground, or escalated
- **Result:** the decision, what shipped, and what you learned

*Good signal:* you use data, not seniority, to resolve disagreements; you disagree and commit.

### 2. "Tell me about a time you owned a project end-to-end."
- **Situation:** the project and why it mattered
- **Task:** your end-to-end responsibility
- **Action:** how you planned, ran, handled setbacks, and shipped
- **Result:** metric impact and what you'd do differently

*Good signal:* you anticipated failure modes, not just the happy path.

### 3. "Tell me about a time you made a mistake / had an incident."
- **Situation:** what went wrong
- **Task:** your role in it
- **Action:** how you responded, mitigated, and prevented recurrence
- **Result:** the fix, the postmortem, and the long-term change

*Good signal:* blameless, owns the mistake, concrete prevention (not "we'll be more careful").

### 4. "Tell me about a time you had to simplify or cut scope."
- **Situation:** a project growing too large or complex
- **Task:** the decision to cut or simplify
- **Action:** how you chose what to cut, communicated with stakeholders
- **Result:** shipped on time, core value preserved

*Good signal:* you say "no" to features to protect the timeline and quality — just like scoping a system design.

## Resume tips for system design roles

- **Lead with impact, not tools.** Instead of "Used Kafka and Redis," say "Reduced p99 latency 40% by adding a caching layer with Redis and offloading events to Kafka."
- **Include scale.** Numbers matter: QPS, users, data volume, cost savings, incidents reduced.
- **Show ownership.** Use verbs like "designed," "led," "owned," "migrated," "reduced," "prevented."
- **One page.** Senior roles still fit on one page; be concise.
- **Link to work.** GitHub, a tech blog, or this guide's case studies you've completed show depth.

## Connecting behavioral to system design

In the design portion, you can weave leadership signals in naturally:

- **Tradeoffs with maturity:** "I'd pick X because the team already operates it well — operational familiarity matters more than theoretical elegance."
- **Incident awareness:** "I'd add a circuit breaker here; I've seen a slow dependency cascade before and it's not pretty."
- **Collaboration:** "I'd write a short tech spec and get it reviewed before building, so we align on the sharding key upfront."

These show you're not just diagramming boxes — you're thinking like a senior engineer.

## Quick prep checklist

- [ ] 3 STAR stories ready (disagreement, ownership, mistake/incident)
- [ ] 1 story about cutting scope or simplifying
- [ ] Resume updated with impact + scale numbers
- [ ] Can explain your hardest technical decision and why
- [ ] Can name a production incident you helped resolve and the fix
